# Lesson plan — M01: Cardiac Arrest and the Chain of Survival

## Audience and level
All clinical staff (Module 1 of the BLS core): ward and theatre nurses, junior doctors, allied
health professionals, nursing students. They know basic cardiac anatomy; many have not yet led
or joined a resuscitation.

## Objectives
By the end the learner can:
1. Define cardiac arrest as an electrical failure of the heart's pumping and distinguish it from
   a heart attack (a circulation problem).
2. Name the four rhythms of cardiac arrest and say which are shockable.
3. State the scale of the problem — survival out of and in hospital — and why the first minutes
   and immediate CPR matter.
4. List the six links of the 2025 unified Chain of Survival in order and say what each means for
   the person at the bedside.
5. Say what changed in 2025, what did not, and what this course certifies.

## Explanation notes
- Scene 2: the ventricles stop contracting in a coordinated way; in fibrillation they quiver, so no
  blood is ejected — the flow arrows disappear. Electrical, not plumbing.
- Scene 3: the misconception "cardiac arrest = heart attack". One is blocked circulation, the
  other a failure of the heart's electrical activity; the response to arrest is immediate CPR.
- Scene 4: the rhythm decides whether a shock can help; non-shockable rhythms are treated with
  CPR and the search for reversible causes (detail in Module 6).
- Scene 5: survival is low but varies with setting — in hospital, nearly a quarter of adults
  survive; the figures argue for immediate action, not fatalism.
- Scene 6: compressions are the heart's substitute pump until defibrillation or recovery.
- Scene 7: one chain for every setting; the first three links are the bedside clinician's job.
- Scene 8: update, don't relearn — the numbers they know still hold.

## Terms and pronunciation
ventricular fibrillation, pulseless ventricular tachycardia, pulseless electrical activity,
asystole, defibrillation, epinephrine (not used here), survivorship. Abbreviations shown on
screen only (VF, PEA, CPR, AED); narration says the full forms.

## Length
≈ 6–6.5 min (≈ 700 narration words at 121 wpm plus holds): five objectives, two flowcharts and
four tables need the extra minute over 5 to explain each properly.
