## Short Description
Combat antimicrobial resistance — appropriate prescribing for doctors, pharmacists and ICU teams.

## Full Description
This course covers the principles of antimicrobial stewardship, empirical versus definitive therapy, de-escalation, dose optimisation, duration, surgical prophylaxis and the use of hospital antibiograms. Rising antimicrobial resistance in Indian hospitals makes prescribing discipline urgent, and NABH 6th Edition expects a functioning stewardship programme. WHO guidance on antibiotic categories supports the prescribing decisions taught through case-based discussion in this course.

## Course Objectives
1. Explain the drivers of antimicrobial resistance in Indian hospital settings
2. Apply empirical prescribing choices guided by local antibiogram and culture data
3. Demonstrate de-escalation, intravenous to oral switch and duration review at the bedside
4. Use audit and feedback methods to monitor antimicrobial use in wards

## Course Learning Outcomes
1. Learners can select an empirical regimen appropriate to the suspected source
2. Learners can review therapy at 48 to 72 hours and de-escalate
3. Learners can identify unnecessary prolonged surgical prophylaxis and correct it
4. Learners can contribute antimicrobial consumption data to the stewardship committee

## Who Can Take This Course
Consultants, Medical Officers, postgraduate residents, intensivists, clinical pharmacists, microbiologists, infection control nurses, ICU and ward nursing staff, and interns posted in medicine or critical care.
