# The Human Heart — fact ledger

Every fact (and every number) the lesson states, with its source. Preflight rejects any
factual number in the lesson that is not written here.

Sources: [T] Tortora & Derrickson, *Principles of Anatomy and Physiology*, 16th ed., ch. 20;
[G] Hall, *Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology*, 14th ed., ch. 9–11;
[M] Marieb & Hoehn, *Human Anatomy & Physiology*, 11th ed., ch. 18.

## Size, position, workload
- The heart lies in the mediastinum, between the lungs, behind the sternum; the base faces
  up and to the right, the apex points down and to the left. [T]
- It is about the size of a closed fist and weighs about 250 to 350 grams (250–350 g). [T][M]
- A resting heart beats about 100,000 times a day (about 100000 beats per day). [T]
- At rest the heart pumps about 5 litres of blood per minute; that is more than 7,000
  litres (7000) a day (5 L × 1440 min ≈ 7200 L). [T][G]
- An adult's total blood volume is about 5 litres. [T]

## Wall (outside → in)
- Fibrous pericardium; parietal layer of the serous pericardium; pericardial cavity with
  pericardial fluid (reduces friction); visceral layer of the serous pericardium =
  epicardium; myocardium (cardiac muscle); endocardium (lines chambers and valves). [T]

## Chambers and valves
- Right atrium receives deoxygenated blood from the superior and inferior venae cavae;
  right ventricle pumps it into the pulmonary trunk; left atrium receives oxygenated blood
  from the pulmonary veins; left ventricle pumps it into the aorta. [T]
- The interventricular septum separates the ventricles. [T]
- Tricuspid valve (right atrioventricular); pulmonary valve; mitral (bicuspid, left
  atrioventricular) valve; aortic valve. [T]
- Chordae tendineae connect the atrioventricular valve cusps to papillary muscles and
  stop the cusps everting into the atria when the ventricles contract. [T]
- The left ventricular wall is about three times thicker than the right. [T]

## Circulations
- Pulmonary circulation: right heart → lungs → left heart. Systemic circulation: left heart
  → body tissues → right heart. They are in series. [T]
- Arteries carry blood away from the heart, veins toward it; the pulmonary arteries carry
  deoxygenated blood and the pulmonary veins carry oxygenated blood. [T]
- Systolic pressure: left ventricle and aorta about 120 mmHg; right ventricle and
  pulmonary artery about 25 mmHg. [G]

## Conduction and rhythm
- The sinoatrial (SA) node in the wall of the right atrium near the superior vena cava is
  the pacemaker; the impulse spreads over both atria → atrioventricular (AV) node, where it
  is delayed about 0.1 seconds (0.1 s) so the atria finish contracting → bundle of His
  (atrioventricular bundle) → right and left bundle branches in the septum → Purkinje
  fibres; the ventricles contract from the apex upward. [T][G]
- Intrinsic rates: SA node 60 to 100 per minute (60–100); AV node 40 to 60 (40–60);
  Purkinje fibres 20 to 40 (20–40). The fastest pacemaker sets the rate. [T][G]
- ECG: P wave = atrial depolarisation; QRS complex = ventricular depolarisation (normally
  under 0.12 seconds, 0.12 s); T wave = ventricular repolarisation; PR interval normally
  0.12 to 0.20 seconds (0.12–0.20 s). [G][T]

## Cardiac cycle
- Atrial systole; isovolumetric contraction (all valves closed; AV valves closing = first
  heart sound S1, "lub"); ventricular ejection (semilunar valves open); isovolumetric
  relaxation (semilunar valves closing = second heart sound S2, "dub"); ventricular filling
  (AV valves open). [T][G]

## Cardiac output
- Cardiac output = heart rate × stroke volume; at rest about 70 beats per minute × about
  70 mL = 4,900 mL/min (4900), about 4.9 litres per minute. [T][G]

## Coronary circulation
- The coronary arteries arise from the aortic root just above the aortic valve. The left
  main (left coronary) artery divides into the left anterior descending (anterior
  interventricular) artery — front of the left ventricle and the septum — and the
  circumflex, which wraps to the side and back of the left ventricle. The right coronary
  artery supplies the right heart and, in most people, the SA and AV nodes. [T][M]
- The left ventricle is perfused mainly during diastole. A blocked coronary artery causes
  ischaemia and myocardial infarction in the muscle beyond it. [T][G]
