## What it is
- Blood pressure is the force exerted by circulating blood against the walls of the body's
  arteries, the major blood vessels in the body. [WHO]
- Hypertension is when blood pressure is too high. [WHO]
- Arteries carry blood away from the heart; their elastic walls stretch as the heart ejects
  blood and recoil between beats, which keeps blood moving. [T]
- Blood pressure is written as two numbers. The first (systolic) number represents the
  pressure in blood vessels when the heart contracts or beats. The second (diastolic) number
  represents the pressure in the vessels when the heart rests between beats. [WHO]
- It is measured in millimetres of mercury (mmHg); a reading such as 120/80 mmHg is said
  "120 over 80". [NHS]
- It is measured with an inflatable cuff placed around the upper arm that inflates and
  squeezes the arm; a digital monitor shows the reading. [NHS]

## What the numbers mean (adults) [NHS]
- Low blood pressure: 90/60 mmHg or lower (90 over 60).
- Ideal blood pressure: between 90/60 mmHg and 120/80 mmHg.
- Readings between 120/80 mmHg and 140/90 mmHg could mean you are at risk of developing high
  blood pressure if you do not take steps to keep it under control.
- High blood pressure: 140/90 mmHg or higher (or an average of 135/85 mmHg at home).
- Diagnosis: hypertension is diagnosed if, when it is measured on two different days, the
  systolic reading on both days is 140 mmHg or higher and/or the diastolic reading on both
  days is 90 mmHg or higher. [WHO]

## Why it matters [WHO]
- Most people with hypertension don't feel any symptoms; checking blood pressure is the
  best way to know if you have high blood pressure.
- An estimated 46% of adults with hypertension are unaware that they have the condition
  (46 percent).
- An estimated 1.28 billion adults aged 30 to 79 years worldwide have hypertension.
- Hypertension can cause serious damage to organs: it can cause chest pain, heart attacks,
  heart failure, irregular heartbeat, and stroke, and it can cause kidney damage.
- With high pressure the heart has to work harder to pump blood. [NHS]
- High blood pressure puts extra strain on your blood vessels, heart and other organs, such as
  the brain, kidneys and eyes; persistent high blood pressure can damage them. [NHS]

## Prevention [WHO]
- Eat more vegetables and fruits; reduce salt to less than 5 g daily (5 grams; just under a
  teaspoon); be physically active; avoid tobacco; limit alcohol; check your blood pressure
  regularly.

## Worked example used in the knowledge check (derived, not a new fact)
- A reading of 150/95 mmHg (150 over 95) on two different days: the systolic value 150 is
  at or above 140 and the diastolic value 95 is at or above 90, so it meets the WHO
  definition of hypertension above.
