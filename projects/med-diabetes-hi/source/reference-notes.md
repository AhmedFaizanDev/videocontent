Sources:

[WHO-DX] WHO/IDF, *Definition and diagnosis of diabetes mellitus and intermediate hyperglycaemia* (2006); WHO, *Use of glycated haemoglobin (HbA1c) in the diagnosis of diabetes mellitus* (2011)

## What it is
- Diabetes is a chronic disease that occurs either when the pancreas does not produce
  enough insulin or when the body cannot effectively use the insulin it produces. [WHO]
- Insulin is a hormone that regulates blood glucose (blood sugar). [WHO]
- Raised blood glucose (hyperglycaemia) is a common effect of uncontrolled diabetes and over
  time leads to serious damage to many of the body's systems, especially the nerves and
  blood vessels. [WHO]

## How insulin works [G]
- After a meal, carbohydrates are digested to glucose, which is absorbed into the blood, so
  blood glucose rises.
- The beta cells of the islets of Langerhans in the pancreas respond by releasing insulin
  into the blood.
- Insulin binds to insulin receptors on the surface of body cells (for example muscle and
  fat cells); this brings glucose transporters to the cell membrane, and glucose moves from
  the blood into the cell, where it is used for energy or stored. Blood glucose then falls.
- Insulin acts like a key: without it (or when cells do not respond to it), glucose stays in
  the blood.

## Types [WHO]
- Type 1 diabetes: characterised by deficient insulin production; requires daily
  administration of insulin. The cause is not known and it is not preventable with current
  knowledge.
- Type 2 diabetes: the body does not use insulin properly (it cannot effectively use the
  insulin it produces). More than 95% of people with diabetes have type 2 diabetes (95
  percent). It is largely the result of excess body weight and physical inactivity.

## Symptoms [WHO]
- Feeling very thirsty; needing to urinate more often than usual; blurred vision; feeling
  tired; losing weight unintentionally.

## Complications [WHO]
- Over time, diabetes can damage blood vessels in the heart, eyes, kidneys and nerves. It
  raises the risk of heart attack, stroke and kidney failure. It can cause permanent vision
  loss by damaging blood vessels in the eyes. Nerve damage and poor blood flow in the feet
  can lead to foot ulcers and amputation.

## Diagnosis (adults, not pregnant) [WHO-DX]
- Fasting plasma glucose 7.0 mmol/L or higher (126 mg/dL or higher; 126 milligrams per
  decilitre).
- HbA1c 6.5% or higher (6.5 percent; 48 mmol/mol) — the average blood glucose over roughly
  the previous two to three months.

## Prevention and control [WHO]
- Keep a healthy body weight; be physically active — at least 30 minutes of regular,
  moderate-intensity activity on most days (30 minutes); eat a healthy diet and avoid sugar
  and saturated fat; do not use tobacco.
- People with diabetes need regular checks of blood glucose; people with type 1 diabetes
  need insulin; many people with type 2 diabetes need medicines.

## Worked example used in the knowledge check (derived, not a new fact)
- A fasting plasma glucose of 140 mg/dL (140 milligrams per decilitre) is above the 126 mg/dL
  threshold above; on its own a single raised result should be confirmed by repeat testing
  before diagnosis [WHO-DX].
