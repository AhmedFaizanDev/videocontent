Cardiac arrest is the only emergency in which the actions of the nearest person in the first
three minutes matter more than everything done by everyone else afterwards. This course
is built around that fact. It is written against the 2025 American Heart Association
Guidelines for CPR and Emergency Cardiovascular Care, released in October 2025 — a
comprehensive revision of the 2020 guidelines.
What this course can and cannot certify
This is a knowledge course. It builds understanding of resuscitation science, the algorithms, and the
hospital system around them, and it produces a MedSkills certificate of completion. It is NOT a
substitute for a hands-on BLS or ACLS provider course with manikin-based skills assessment, and it
does not confer an AHA, ERC or equivalent provider card. Compressions, bag-mask ventilation and
defibrillator operation are psychomotor skills that must be assessed physically at an authorised
training centre. Use this course to prepare for and reinforce that training — not to replace it.

How the course is organised
Eleven modules, issued as separate documents so departments can assign what each role needs.
Modules 1 to 5 are the BLS core and are for every clinical staff member. Modules 6 to 11 carry the
advanced life support, peri-arrest, post-arrest and systems content for those who respond to or lead
codes.

Total course duration: 13 hours
Comprising approximately 9 hours of self-paced module study, 2.5 hours of applied practice and
scenario work, and 1.5 hours of assessment. This is in addition to the hands-on provider course and
skills assessment required for BLS or ACLS certification.

A note on the 2025 Guidelines
The AHA released the 2025 Guidelines for CPR and ECC on 22 October 2025. If you trained on the
2020 guidelines, most of what you know remains correct — you are updating, not relearning. The
compression rate of 100 to 120 per minute, the 30:2 ratio for single-rescuer adult CPR, the reversible
causes and the overall shape of the algorithms all still hold.
What changed matters, though. There is now a single unified Chain of Survival covering adults and
children, in-hospital and out-of-hospital. The terminology has been revised: ‘rescue breaths’ is no
longer used, ‘breaths’ now refers to assisted breathing, and ‘ventilation’ is reserved for mechanical
respiratory support. Opioid antagonists have entered the BLS algorithm for healthcare professionals,
there is a new choking algorithm, and in shockable rhythms epinephrine is now positioned after initial
defibrillation attempts have failed rather than alongside them. Vasopressin is no longer
recommended as a substitute for epinephrine. Each module notes where the 2025 position differs
from 2020.

A note on learning hours
NABH does not prescribe a fixed number of training hours for any individual topic. What the 6th
Edition requires is that the organisation identifies training needs, provides training including
resuscitation training for relevant staff, and maintains records of the date, duration and effectiveness
of that training. The hours above are MedSkills’ recommended durations, recorded automatically
against each learner in the LMS. Your hospital’s own resuscitation policy will set the certification and
recertification interval, and that is binding.

Assessment and certification
•

Each module carries a knowledge check written in the style of the final assessment.

•

The final assessment covers all eleven modules. The pass mark and permitted attempts are set by
your institution.

•

On passing, a verifiable MedSkills certificate of knowledge completion is issued, with date and
duration written to your training record.

•

This certificate records knowledge, not psychomotor skill. Your provider card must come from a
hands-on course at a recognised training centre.

What you should be able to do at the end
1

Recognise cardiac arrest within seconds and start compressions without waiting for permission or
for the team.

2

Describe high-quality CPR precisely — rate, depth, recoil, interruptions — and explain why each
parameter matters.

3

Operate an AED and describe safe manual defibrillation, including pad placement and minimising
the peri-shock pause.

4

Follow the adult cardiac arrest algorithm and state the drugs, doses and timing.

How to use these documents
•

Read the modules in order; each builds on the previous one.

•

Attempt every knowledge check before reading the answers.

•

Print the algorithm figures and post them beside the resuscitation trolley.

•

Bring this material to your hands-on provider course; the psychomotor training is far more effective
when the reasoning is already in place.

•

Practise. Skills decay within months. Brief, frequent practice beats a long course every two years.
Currency and local protocol
This content is current as at August 2026 and is based on the AHA 2025 Guidelines. Resuscitation
guidance is revised periodically and regional councils — including the European Resuscitation
Council and the Indian Resuscitation Council — publish their own guidance which may differ in
detail. Drug doses, energy settings and targets must always be checked against your own hospital’s
resuscitation policy and your own device’s recommended settings. Where your hospital protocol
differs from this material, follow the hospital protocol and raise the discrepancy with your
resuscitation committee.

Figures in this course
All diagrams are original works created for MedSkills. No third-party stock imagery is used, which
means the algorithms, doses and parameters shown are drawn to match the guidelines they cite
rather than approximating them. They may be reproduced within the licensed institution for training
and display purposes.
