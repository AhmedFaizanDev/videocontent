# Hand hygiene: the five moments — fact ledger (lesson narrated in Marathi)

Every fact (and every number) the lesson states, with its source. Preflight rejects any
factual number in the lesson that is not written here. Written in English; the lesson
states these facts in Marathi.

Sources:
[WHO-G] World Health Organization, *WHO Guidelines on Hand Hygiene in Health Care* (2009)
[WHO-5] WHO, *Your 5 Moments for Hand Hygiene* (poster and information leaflet, 2009)
[WHO-R] WHO, *How to Handrub?* poster (2009)
[WHO-W] WHO, *How to Handwash?* poster (2009)
[TAY] Taylor LJ. An evaluation of handwashing techniques. *Nursing Times* 1978 (the areas of
the hands most often missed, reproduced widely in hand-hygiene training)

## Why [WHO-G]
- Health care-associated infections are a major cause of harm to patients.
- The hands of health workers are the most common vehicle for transmitting health
  care-associated germs from patient to patient and within the care environment.
- Hand hygiene is the single most important measure to reduce these infections.

## The patient zone [WHO-5]
- The patient zone is the patient and their immediate surroundings: for example the bed,
  the bedside table, and the equipment and objects touched by the patient.

## The five moments [WHO-5]
1. Before touching a patient — to protect the patient from germs carried on your hands.
2. Before a clean or aseptic procedure — for example before handling a cannula or a dressing.
3. After body fluid exposure risk — for example after contact with blood, urine or a drainage bag.
4. After touching a patient — to protect yourself and the care environment.
5. After touching patient surroundings — even if you did not touch the patient.
- Who each moment protects [WHO-5]: moments 1 and 2 (before) protect the patient against
  harmful germs — carried on your hands, or (moment 2) including the patient's own germs
  entering the body; moments 3, 4 and 5 (after) protect you and the health-care environment
  from harmful patient germs.

## Which method [WHO-G]
- Use an alcohol-based handrub as the routine method when hands are not visibly soiled.
- Wash hands with soap and water when they are visibly dirty or soiled with blood or other
  body fluids.

## Technique
- Handrub: the whole procedure takes 20 to 30 seconds (20–30 seconds). [WHO-R]
- Handwash: the whole procedure takes 40 to 60 seconds (40–60 seconds). [WHO-W]
- Handrub steps [WHO-R]: apply a palmful and cover all surfaces; rub palm to palm; right
  palm over the back of the left hand with fingers interlaced, and vice versa; palm to palm
  with fingers interlaced; backs of the fingers to the opposing palm with fingers
  interlocked; rotational rubbing of each thumb clasped in the opposite palm; rotational
  rubbing of the clasped fingertips in the opposite palm, backwards and forwards; once dry,
  hands are safe.
- Areas of the hands most often missed: the thumbs, the fingertips and between the
  fingers. [TAY]
