Source: `source/reference-notes.md` (fact ledger). One lesson, exactly 5:00, en-GB.

## AS01 — Asthma: What Happens in the Airways

**Objectives:** what asthma is (chronic airway inflammation) · where it happens (the
bronchioles) · what happens in an attack (muscle tightens, lining swells, mucus builds —
airway narrows, airflow drops) · common symptoms · common triggers · the two inhaler kinds
(reliever vs preventer) · a common mistake · check understanding.

Scenes (≈12):

1. **intro** — names the topic, then lists what we'll cover: what asthma is, where it happens,
   what happens in an attack, and how inhalers help.
2. **stage (hook)** — `person` out and about, breathing normally, then meets a trigger and
   starts to wheeze and clutch the chest; `person.worry`, label "Something's changed" — sets up
   the question "what's happening inside?"
3. **stage (what it is / where)** — camera dives from `lungs` overview down the trachea and
   bronchi; an `inset` loupe zooms from `lungs.bronchi` into the new `bronchiole` cross-section;
   note names asthma as chronic inflammation of the airways living in the bronchioles.
4. **stage (the mechanism)** — on `bronchiole`: `constrict` (the muscle ring around the wall
   tightens), `inflame` (the mucosa swells), `mucus` (thick mucus appears in the lumen) — three
   beats, each labelled on its anchor — then a `travel` beat shows the air stream squeezing
   through the narrowed `bronchiole.lumen`, thinner than before; note "airway narrows, less air
   gets through."
5. **stage (symptoms)** — back on `person`: four beats label wheeze (mouth/chest), breathlessness
   (chest heaving), chest tightness (hand on chest), cough (`person` doubles forward) — one
   symptom per sentence, each pinned on the body.
6. **stage (triggers, as stations)** — a row the camera visits: `icon` (cigarette) for smoke,
   `icon` (walk) for exercise, plus notes for allergens (pollen, dust mites), cold air, and
   respiratory infections as the camera pans past each "station"; `bronchiole` sits small in
   the corner and gives a quick `constrict` pulse each time a trigger is named.
7. **stage (reliever inhaler)** — new `inhaler` part: `puff` releases its mist over `person`'s
   mouth; camera cuts to `bronchiole`, `relieve` fires — the muscle ring relaxes and the lumen
   reopens; note "reliever: relaxes the muscle, fast."
8. **stage (preventer inhaler)** — a second `inhaler` (taken daily, at home, not mid-symptom);
   over several beats `bronchiole.inflame` visibly eases and stays down; note "preventer: taken
   every day, calms inflammation over time."
9. **mistake** — wrong: "I feel fine, so I stopped my preventer, and just use the reliever
   when I need it" (`cross` the preventer being skipped); right: keep taking the preventer daily
   even when well, and get inhaler technique checked.
10. **check** — visual: `bronchiole` mid-attack (all three changes shown); question asks what's
    narrowing this airway.
11. **answer** — same `bronchiole` visual, three points fire in turn: muscle tightened, lining
    swollen, mucus added.
12. **recap** — bullets: chronic inflammation of the airways — bronchoconstriction, swelling,
    mucus narrow it; reliever relaxes fast, preventer calms it daily; takeaway: know your
    triggers, use both inhalers as prescribed.
