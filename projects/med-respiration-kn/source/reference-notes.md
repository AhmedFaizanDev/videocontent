# Breathing and gas exchange — fact ledger (lesson narrated in Kannada)

states these facts in Kannada.

Sources:

## The airway [T]
- Air passes through the nose (or mouth), pharynx and larynx into the trachea (windpipe),
  which divides into the right and left primary bronchi; these branch again and again into
  smaller bronchioles that end in tiny air sacs, the alveoli.
- C-shaped rings of cartilage keep the trachea open.
- The lungs contain about 300 million alveoli (300 million), giving a very large surface —
  about 70 square metres (70) — for gas exchange.

## Breathing (pulmonary ventilation) [T]
- Inhalation: the diaphragm, a dome-shaped sheet of muscle under the lungs, contracts and
  flattens (moves down); the external intercostal muscles lift the ribs. The chest cavity
  gets bigger, pressure inside the lungs falls below the air pressure outside, and air
  flows in.
- The diaphragm is the most important muscle of inhalation: during quiet breathing its
  contraction accounts for about 75% of the air that enters the lungs (75 percent).
- Exhalation at rest is passive: the diaphragm and intercostals relax, the diaphragm domes
  upward, the elastic lungs recoil, the chest cavity gets smaller, pressure in the lungs
  rises above the outside pressure, and air flows out.
- A healthy adult at rest breathes about 12 to 20 times a minute (12–20 breaths per minute). [CC]

## Gas exchange [T]
- Atmospheric air is about 21% oxygen (20.9%; about 21 percent). [T]
- In the alveoli, oxygen and carbon dioxide move by diffusion, each from where its partial
  pressure is higher to where it is lower.
- Oxygen diffuses from the alveolar air into the blood of the pulmonary capillaries; carbon
  dioxide diffuses from the capillary blood into the alveolar air, and is breathed out.
- The alveolar wall and the capillary wall are each a single thin layer of cells; together
  they form the respiratory membrane, only about 0.5 micrometres thick (0.5), so diffusion
  is fast.
- In the blood, oxygen is carried mostly by haemoglobin inside red blood cells; blood
  arriving at the lungs is deoxygenated and leaves oxygenated.
- Carbon dioxide is a waste product of the body's cells.
